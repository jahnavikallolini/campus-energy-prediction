### 9.1 Interpretation
1. **Validation selection.** Support Vector Regression has the lowest macro-average validation nRMSE_std (0.321; macro R2 0.892) and wins 5 of 7 buildings; Linear Regression (0.348), Decision Tree (0.357) and KNN (0.398) follow. The persistence baseline scores 0.442, so SVR removes about 27% of its standardised RMSE and Linear Regression about 21%. The seasonal-naive baseline (0.710) is much worse. Per protocol the selected model is therefore **SVR**, and that selection was made before any test metric was computed.
2. **Test performance (frozen models, evaluated once).** Every model is worse than on validation: SVR 0.450, Linear Regression 0.421, Decision Tree 0.480, KNN 0.517 (persistence 0.486). On the full test period Linear Regression has the lowest macro error, so the validation and test rankings differ. The selection is not changed on the basis of test results.
3. **Why the ranking flips: a timestamp problem (section 9.2, item 1).** Splitting the test period at the break found in the energy timestamps (about 2017-07-08), the primary-variant macro nRMSE_std is: before the break SVR 0.382, Linear Regression 0.392, Decision Tree 0.465, KNN 0.464; after it SVR 0.503, Decision Tree 0.477, KNN 0.554, Linear Regression 0.446. On the clean part SVR is again the best model, consistent with validation. Models that lean on hour-of-day and calendar features degrade after the break, whereas persistence (0.490 before, 0.477 after) and mostly lag-driven Linear Regression do not. Even before the break the test error (0.38 to 0.46) is above the validation error (0.32 to 0.40), so a part of the degradation is ordinary period-to-period variation (test covers January to July with almost no February data and a different seasonal mix than validation).
4. **Margins are modest.** SVR beats Linear Regression by 0.027 nRMSE_std on validation and by 0.010 on the pre-break test period. SVR needs about 56 s to train seven buildings against 0.2 s for Linear Regression and is the least interpretable. The tuned SVR sits at the edge of its grid (C = 30, gamma = 0.005, the lowest gamma tried) in a flat landscape, i.e. close to a smooth, almost linear fit.
5. **Per building.** Academic is the most predictable (validation R2 0.96 with SVR; test 0.91 with Linear Regression). Library and Facilities are next (validation R2 0.93 and 0.91 with SVR). Girls Hostel, Boys Hostel and Mess are the hardest (validation R2 of the best model 0.85, 0.85 and 0.84; test R2 0.71 to 0.81 across models). Pooled MAE/RMSE in watts are dominated by Academic, Mess and Boys Hostel and should not be used alone.
6. **Occupancy adds no measurable value in this setup.** On identical rows (P vs E_matched), validation nRMSE_std is Linear 0.348 vs 0.350, Decision Tree 0.357 vs 0.355, SVR 0.321 vs 0.317, KNN 0.398 vs 0.372; on test Linear 0.421 vs 0.422, Decision Tree 0.480 vs 0.472, SVR 0.450 vs 0.438, KNN 0.517 vs 0.468. Energy-only is equal or better in 6 of 8 comparisons. Lagged power already carries the occupancy-driven load pattern, and extra distance-based inputs hurt KNN. This does not show that occupancy is unrelated to load (same-hour Spearman correlations in the EDA are 0.32 to 0.70), only that lagged occupancy gives no extra predictive skill beyond power lags. After the timestamp break the occupancy lags are also misaligned with the shifted energy series.
7. **More training rows help slightly.** Training on all energy-only rows (E_all) and scoring on the common rows gives test nRMSE_std Decision Tree 0.467, KNN 0.466, SVR 0.441, Linear 0.421 versus P 0.480, 0.517, 0.450, 0.421.
8. **Lecture (zero-inflated).** In the test period 66% of Lecture hours (3,335) have exactly 0 W. The best model predicts zero-target hours well in absolute terms (mean prediction 58 to 128 W, MAE 58 to 128 W), while MAE in non-zero hours is 686 to 778 W; zero-target hours contribute only 6 to 15% of the squared error. R2 for Lecture is therefore driven by the on/off transitions and the non-zero regime. Linear Regression and SVR produce negative raw predictions for Lecture (902 and 930 validation rows, 1,400 and 1,205 test rows), which were clipped to 0 W; Decision Tree never does. Test R2 for Lecture ranges from 0.70 (Decision Tree) to 0.82 (Linear Regression), against 0.88 to 0.92 on validation. For anomaly detection, residuals for Lecture are strongly heteroscedastic (tiny in zero regimes, wide when the building is on), so a single residual threshold per building would be poorly calibrated there.
9. **Encoding.** One-hot encoding of hour, day of week and month was selected on validation for Linear Regression, KNN and SVR in both feature sets; cyclic encoding was competitive but not selected. The Decision Tree uses raw integers.

### 9.2 Problems discovered
1. **Energy timestamps appear shifted by about 5 h from July 2017 onward (all seven buildings).**
   - Monthly daily-profile alignment is 0 h (within +-2 h) for 2013 to June 2017, then 4 to 6 h for July to December 2017 in every building (fig19; `timestamp_shift_monthly_profile_shift_hours.csv`).
   - The jump is abrupt: the Academic rising-edge hour moves from 8-9 h to 3-4 h between 2017-07-07 and 2017-07-10, right after a long logging gap (742 min at 2017-07-08 12:21 for Academic and Lecture, 971 min at 16:10 for Mess, a 33 h gap on 2017-07-10 for Library).
   - Occupancy timestamps do not shift (all seven profiles stay at 0 +-1 h).
   - Against occupancy, the best energy shift is 0 to 50 min before the break but 270 to 320 min after it (Spearman with occupancy for Academic 0.19 at zero shift vs 0.87 at +320 min).
   - Cause not established (a logger clock or time-zone handling change is plausible). Nothing was corrected: the raw data, the processed data and the models are untouched.
   - Impact: 16,885 of the 37,264 primary test rows (45%) fall after the break; time-of-day models and the occupancy lag features are unreliable there, and anomaly timestamps would be wrong.
2. **SVR hyperparameters sit at the edge of the grid** (after one extension of the grid on validation; the first grid had its optimum at C = 3 and gamma = 0.02, also an edge). Differences between neighbouring settings are small (about 0.002 to 0.01 nRMSE_std).
3. **The persistence baseline is strong.** KNN is worse than persistence on the full test period (0.517 vs 0.486) and Decision Tree is about equal (0.480).
4. **Predictions needed clipping at 0 W** (almost entirely Lecture; Library 3 and 5 rows).
5. **Test months are uneven**: almost no February 2017 rows (occupancy outage), no December, and validation covers only July to December 2016.
6. **Frozen models are fitted on the training rows only** (not refitted on train + validation), so the six months nearest to the test period are not used for training. This keeps validation and test numbers comparable but may cost accuracy.
7. **Standardised errors use the training-period sigma.** nRMSE_std for Lecture is influenced by its changing zero share; use it together with R2, MAE and the zero-hour table.

### 9.3 Decisions needed before anomaly detection
1. **Timestamp break.** Options: (a) verify against your master copy and the dataset authors whether the shift exists in the original files; (b) keep the data as is and report results for the full test period and for the pre-break test period separately; (c) restrict the primary test period to 2017-01-01 to 2017-07-07 (changes the agreed split end date); (d) apply a documented correction of about +5 to +5.5 h to energy timestamps after the break (needs a verified offset; not done). My recommendation is (a) first, then (c) if the shift is confirmed and cannot be corrected with confidence, with the full-period results kept as a sensitivity analysis.
2. **Model for anomaly detection.** The validation rule selects SVR. If you accept a lower-complexity alternative, Linear Regression is within 0.027 nRMSE_std on validation and more stable after the break, but that would deviate from the stated rule.
3. **Primary matrix.** Given item 6 of 9.1, confirm whether the anomaly stage should use the primary matrix (energy + lagged occupancy, as specified) or also the energy-only variant.
